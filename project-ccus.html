Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage with ESG

Strategic Integration within Global ESG Frameworks

Overview

This research examines how Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage (CCUS) technologies can be strategically integrated within Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) frameworks to accelerate sustainable climate solutions.

The 2026 Landscape

CCUS has crossed from pilots into early commercial reality. The Global CCS Institute's latest Global Status of CCS report counts 77 projects in operation — a 54% jump in a single year — with 47 more under construction adding 44 million tonnes of annual capture capacity, and 610 projects in development. Total capacity across operating and planned projects has grown 23% to over 500 million tonnes per year. Yet the market is still splitting in two: a small number of large, integrated hubs advance on solid economics and government backing, while many standalone projects stall without mandates or offtake guarantees. This bifurcation makes the governance pillar more decisive than ever.

Carbon Border Pricing

The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism entered into force in 2026, extending carbon pricing to imports and strengthening the business case for capture in heavy industry

Storage Networks Go Commercial

Norway's Northern Lights — the first open-access cross-border CO₂ transport and storage network — injected its first CO₂ in August 2025 and is building out a second phase to lift capacity to around 5 million tonnes per year, alongside Denmark's Greensand hub

A DAC Reality Check

Stratos in Texas, designed as the world's largest direct air capture plant at 500,000 tonnes per year, missed its Q2 2026 startup after a commissioning fault — while BECCS has overtaken DAC in the carbon removal project pipeline. Removal at scale is coming, but first-of-a-kind timelines slip

National Funding Rounds

Germany's industrial decarbonisation programme of about €6 billion rolls out competitive support mechanisms from mid 2026
